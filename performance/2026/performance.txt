Statement of Accomplishments – Zilinghan Li

TIME PERIOD:  October 1, 2025 through September 30, 2026 (FY26)
Summary
During fiscal year 2026, my work focused on turning privacy-preserving federated learning (FL) into production-grade infrastructure for science and on developing AI for biomedicine. As lead developer of the Advanced Privacy-Preserving Federated Learning (APPFL) framework, I evolved it into an enterprise-level platform deployed on NERSC Spin, with secure authentication, multi-user Globus Compute endpoints, and a web gateway for launching on-demand federations. I also contributed to FL on the American Science Cloud and to cross-facility FL among DOE supercomputers, including a queue-aware scheduling algorithm accepted at ICML 2026, and supervised the development of HiveWatch, a new observability toolkit for FL. In AI for biomedicine, my collaboration with Stony Brook University on histopathology foundation models produced papers at NeurIPS 2026, CVPR 2026 Findings, TMLR, as well as a Best Paper Award at the DataCV Workshop at CVPR 2026. I also expanded my mentoring role, supervising three graduate students and onboarding two pre-doctoral researchers.

Software and equipment developed
•	Continued leading the development of the open source APPFL framework with 111 commits (23,103 lines of code added, and 6,030 lines of code removed), including 4 version releases on PyPI in the past fiscal year. Integrated new features such as support for memory-efficient communication for large foundation models, privacy-enhancing algorithms, multi-user Globus Compute endpoints, virtual simulator, Keycloak authentication, agentic skills, federated GWAS, and integration into NERSC Spin. APPFL has received more than 180 stars and 40 forks on GitHub. 
•	Contributed to the design and the development of trac.appflx.link and forge.appflx.link, which host a web platform to authenticate users through Keycloak, create secure federations, and launch on-demand federated learning servers with different backends (gRPC, Globus Compute, and IRI). 
•	Supervised the design and development of HiveWatch from scratch, a monitoring and visualization toolkit for distributed and federated training experiments. Over the past year, I have contributed 21 commits (7,129 lines of code added, and 12,863 lines of code removed), including 2 version releases on PyPI. 
•	Contributed to the development of Apeiron with 6 commits (6,121 lines of code added and 07 lines of code removed). Apeiron is a continual learning framework that automatically detects concept drift in data streams and adapts models through JVP-regularized retraining, as a research product of the ModCon BASE SIM (self-improving model) team. 
•	Contributed to the development of RADAR-II with 5 commits (4,049 lines of code added and 461 lines of code removed). RADAR-II is a federated, privacy-enhancing framework for radio follow-up of gravitational-wave events with features including accelerated federated inference, LLM-assisted GCN parsing, and agentic VLA scheduling.
