Zilinghan joined Argonne as a Graduate Visiting Student in January 2023 and transitioned to a full-time Machine Learning Engineer position in May 2024. Research Contributions and Technical Leadership

Zilinghan's most substantial contribution is his role as the primary architect and main developer of APPFL (Advanced Privacy-Preserving Federated Learning), Argonne's open-source federated learning framework. When Zilinghan joined the project, APPFL was a limited proof-of-concept. Under his technical leadership, he transformed it into a production-grade ecosystem used by researchers across multiple institutions. He designed and built APPFLx, a web service platform that allows domain scientists to participate in federated learning experiments without deep infrastructure expertise, and subsequently led a complete architectural redesign of the APPFL framework to support heterogeneous computing environments, broader security requirements, and a wider range of scientific applications. APPFL now has 176 GitHub stars, 35 forks, and an active Discord community of 67 users. More importantly, it has been adopted and actively deployed at four additional U.S. DOE national laboratories — Oak Ridge, Lawrence Berkeley, Sandia, and Los Alamos — and has been integrated into NIH Bridge2AI's flagship biomedical AI programs.

Beyond framework development, Zilinghan has developed a series of novel federated learning algorithms addressing specific bottlenecks in real-world scientific deployments. He independently conceived and led the development of FedCompass, which addresses the straggler problem in heterogeneous cross-silo FL by dynamically estimating each site's computing capacity and assigning personalized workloads. Published at ICLR 2024 — one of the top three machine learning conferences globally — it has accumulated 31 citations. He also contributed to FedSZ (communication-efficient FL via lossy compression, ICDCS 2024), FedSpaLLM (federated pruning of large language models, NAACL 2025), FedCostAware (cost-aware cloud FL with >70% cost reduction, Allerton 2025), and FedQueue (queue-aware FL for cross-facility HPC, ICML 2026). The last of these represents a significant technical milestone — a paper accepted at ICML 2026, one of the field's top venues, addressing a problem unique to DOE computing environments.

Zilinghan has also made meaningful contributions in biomedical AI. He co-led the development of an open-source LLM pipeline for screening patients with rare genetic aortopathies — rare heritable disorders that go undiagnosed for years in many patients — by parsing free-text clinical notes in electronic health records using open-source models deployable within hospital infrastructure. He served as co-first author on this work, which is under review at Cell Med. He also serves as the primary Argonne liaison for an ongoing collaboration with Stony Brook University on computational pathology, where he has contributed to a series of projects: TICON (slide-level tile contextualizer, CVPR Findings 2026), PixCell (first generative foundation model for digital histopathology, under review at IEEE TPAMI; downloaded over 50,000 times on Hugging Face), Gen-SIS (self-supervised learning via diffusion-generated augmentation, DataCV@CVPR 2026 Best Paper Award), and a study on pathology image compression. He has also contributed to a large-scale genome-wide pleiotropy analysis currently under review at Nature.

On the AI for science front, Zilinghan serves as a key technical contributor on the AuroraGPT post-training team, leading the scaling of supervised fine-tuning (SFT), direct preference optimization (DPO), and Kahneman-Tversky Optimization (KTO) pipelines on the Aurora exascale supercomputer — work that requires significant systems engineering on Intel's unique HPC architecture. He has also contributed to the RADAR project (published in The Astrophysical Journal Supplement Series), a federated AI system for coordinating gravitational-wave electromagnetic follow-up, and is a contributor to ModCon (Transformational AI Models Consortium), a multi-laboratory DOE consortium mandated by the OBBB Act spanning twelve national laboratories.

His work has accumulated 254 total citations, an h-index of 10, and an i10-index of 10 as of May 2026 — a citation profile that is remarkable for a researcher who joined Argonne full-time only two years ago. Multiple recent preprints and workshop papers are already accumulating citations, and several manuscripts are under review at flagship venues including Nature, IEEE TPAMI, and TMLR.
