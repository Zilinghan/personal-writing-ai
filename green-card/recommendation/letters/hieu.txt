Dear Sir/Madam,
I am pleased to write this letter of recommendation for Zilinghan Li, a leading member of the field of computer science. I am Hieu Le, an Assistant Professor at the University of North Carolina at Charlotte, where I specialize in research related to computer vision, medical imaging, and generative foundation models. In terms of my expertise, I earned my Ph.D. in Computer Science from Stony Brook University in 2020 as well as my B.Sc. in the same from Vietnam’s University of Science in 2012. I also have extensive research experience at institutions such as Korea’s POSTECH, France’s Ecole Centrale de Lyon, New York’s Stony Brook University, and Switzerland’s EPFL, as well as industry experience as an applied scientist at Amazon Robotics. Because I have never collaborated with Mr. Li in a direct capacity, I am able to provide an independent and expert evaluation of his research expertise and his noteworthy contributions to our shared field to date. 
Mr. Li’s contributions to AI for digital histopathology draw on core computer vision techniques, including generative modeling, self-supervised representation learning, and spatial context modeling, and his execution of each reflects a strong command of the underlying methodology. As an expert in these techniques myself, I am well positioned to evaluate both the technical rigor and the broader significance of his work within the computer vision community. The field presents distinctive challenges rooted in scale: whole-slide images (WSIs) are gigapixel images that cannot be processed end-to-end by standard deep learning pipelines. The conventional tile-based approach, which divides images into smaller patches, is necessary but discards spatial context that is often diagnostically critical. Mr. Li and his collaborators addressed this with TICON (CVPR Findings 2026), a tile contextualizer that enriches individual tile embeddings with information from surrounding regions, restoring spatial relationships that tile-based pipelines otherwise lose. The work’s acceptance at CVPR, one of the most selective venues in computer vision, is itself a meaningful indicator of quality.
A second persistent challenge in the field is data scarcity. Expert-annotated pathology data is expensive and slow to acquire, making self-supervised and generative approaches essential research directions. Mr. Li contributed to two complementary solutions. PixCell is a generative foundation model for histopathology, pretrained on over 30 million patches from 69,000 whole-slide images, a scale of pathology-specific pretraining that is rare in the field. The model is capable of producing realistic synthetic pathology images that can augment training sets and support low-resource settings. Gen-SIS, another work contributed by Mr. Li, takes a different but complementary approach, training a domain-specific diffusion model to generate augmented views for self-supervised representation learning, thereby improving learned representations without requiring additional labeled data. Gen-SIS was awarded Best Paper at the 5th DataCV Workshop at CVPR 2026 — a competitive venue specifically focused on data challenges in computer vision, where the bar for recognition is high. Rounding out these contributions is a WSI compression study that repurposes autoencoders from latent diffusion models for efficient WSI storage, a practical engineering contribution, given that the global volume of WSI data is measured in petabytes, and compression without degrading diagnostic fidelity remains an open challenge.
Beyond the computational pathology domain, Mr. Li’s broader research output reflects the same pattern of sustained, high-quality contribution. His publication record spans more than 20 articles at major venues including CVPR, ICLR, ICML, and NAACL, as well as journals such as Transactions on Machine Learning Research (TMLR). These are among the most selective and competitive venues in machine learning and computer vision, with acceptance rates consistently below 30%, and consistent publication across them is a reliable indicator of research quality and breadth. His citation record has collectively accumulated around 300 citations, with individual papers drawing up to 46 citations each, which is a strong indicator that his contributions are actively engaged with by the broader research community rather than simply published and set aside.
In sum, Mr. Li’s contributions in TICON, PixCell, and Gen-SIS represent exactly the kind of foundational, practically grounded research the field needs — directly confronting the technical bottlenecks of scale, data scarcity, and representation quality rather than working around them. As a researcher at the intersection of computer vision and medical imaging, I can attest that these are genuinely hard problems, and that recognition at venues like CVPR and through a Best Paper award at DataCV reflects meaningful community validation. His work forms a cohesive body of contributions that advances the state of AI for digital pathology, and I have every reason to believe it will continue to shape the field. I give him my strongest endorsement without reservation.
